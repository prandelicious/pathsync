Question 8: What belongs on each live worker row?

The worker row is the highest-frequency line in the UI. Every field has to earn its space.

Workers
⠋ T01  hashing    [██████████        ]  A001_C014_0101AB.MP4      8.2 GB   78.4 MB/s   T7
⠙ T02  copying    [████████          ]  A001_C015_0101AB.MP4      7.9 GB   64.0 MB/s   Archive
⠹ T03  verifying  [██                ]  GX010193.MP4              2.1 GB   41.8 MB/s   T7

A. Phase + Bar + File + Size + Rate + Target

Dense but complete. Target is a final column, not parenthesized into the rate field.

Workers
⠋ T01  hashing    A001_C014_0101AB.MP4      8.2 GB   78.4 MB/s   T7
⠙ T02  copying    A001_C015_0101AB.MP4      7.9 GB   64.0 MB/s   Archive
⠹ T03  verifying  GX010193.MP4              2.1 GB   41.8 MB/s   T7

B. No Worker Bar

More filename room. Loses per-file progress, especially useful for long hashing/copying phases.

Workers
⠋ T01  T7       hashing    [██████████        ]  A001_C014_0101AB.MP4      8.2 GB   78.4 MB/s
⠙ T02  Archive  copying    [████████          ]  A001_C015_0101AB.MP4      7.9 GB   64.0 MB/s
⠹ T03  T7       verifying  [██                ]  GX010193.MP4              2.1 GB   41.8 MB/s

C. Target Near Worker ID

Emphasizes destination ownership, but pushes phase/bar/file to the right.

My recommendation: A

  • The per-file bar is valuable for large files and hashing phases.
  • Putting target as its own final column keeps filenames more recognizable than parenthesized suffixes.
  • The visual scan order is natural: worker, state, progress, file, size/rate, destination.

Tradeoff

  • On narrow terminals, this needs adaptive truncation and may shorten the bar first.
